Sonometer: frequency of a tuning fork

A stretched wire resonates with a fork when its vibrating length L fits the fork's frequency. Since f × L is constant for a fixed tension, a fork of unknown frequency can be found from its resonant length.

The idea

A wire of linear density μ under tension T carries waves at speed v = √(T/μ). Between two bridges it vibrates in its fundamental mode, so the length is half a wavelength. When the wire's natural frequency equals the fork's, the wire resonates and the paper rider is thrown off.

  • Fundamental: f = v / 2L = (1 / 2L) √(T/μ)
  • Fixed T: f × L = constant = v / 2
  • Unknown fork: fx = (f × L) / Lx

Wire: steel, diameter 0.25 mm, μ = 3.8 × 10⁻⁴ kg/m. Hanger mass 0.5 kg, each slotted weight 0.5 kg, g = 9.8 m/s².

Graph

Plot f against 1/L for the known forks. This is a straight line through the origin whose slope is f × L, and the wave speed is v = 2 × slope.

Record at least 2 known forks to see the best-fit line.

Precautions

  • The wire must be uniform, free from kinks, and stretched taut by the weights.
  • The pulley must turn freely so that the hanging weight really sets the tension.
  • Press the stem of the fork firmly on the box, close to the wire, so the vibration passes into the wire.
  • Move the bridge slowly near resonance: the rider jumps off over a range of only a few millimetres.
  • Read the length from the same edge of the bridge every time, with your eye directly above the scale.
  • Do not change the weights while taking a set of readings.
