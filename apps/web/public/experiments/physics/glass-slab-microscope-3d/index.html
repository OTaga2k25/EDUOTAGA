Glass slab: refractive index with a travelling microscope

A mark seen through a glass slab looks raised. Focus a travelling microscope on the mark, on its image through the slab, and on powder on top of the slab. Then n = real thickness / apparent thickness = (R₃ − R₁) / (R₃ − R₂).

The idea

Light from a mark under a glass slab bends away from the normal as it leaves the top surface, so the mark appears raised. The real thickness of the slab is t, and the apparent thickness (the depth at which the mark seems to lie) is t/n.

  • R₁: microscope focused on the mark (no slab)
  • R₂: focused on the image of the mark seen through the slab
  • R₃: focused on the powder on top of the slab
  • Real thickness = R₃ − R₁, apparent thickness = R₃ − R₂
  • Refractive index: n = (R₃ − R₁) / (R₃ − R₂); the mark is raised by R₂ − R₁ = t(1 − 1/n)

Reading the vernier: the main scale reading is the last main scale mark at or below the vernier zero. Then find the vernier line that lines up exactly with a main scale line: that number is the vernier coincidence. Reading = MSR + VSC × LC, with LC = 1 MSD / 50 = 0.05/50 = 0.001 cm.

    Precautions

    • Focus the eyepiece on the cross-wires first, so they look sharp, before focusing on the mark.
    • Move the microscope in one direction only when focusing (always upwards, say) to avoid backlash error in the screw.
    • Remove parallax: the image of the mark should not move against the cross-wires when you move your eye slightly.
    • Use a very thin layer of fine powder, just enough to focus on the top surface.
    • Read the vernier carefully: note the main scale reading below the vernier zero, then the vernier line that coincides exactly.
    • Repeat with slabs of different thickness and take the mean of n.
