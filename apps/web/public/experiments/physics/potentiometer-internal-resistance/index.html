Potentiometer: internal resistance of a cell

Find the balance length with the cell on open circuit (l₁), then with a resistance R across it (l₂). The cell's internal resistance is r = R (l₁ − l₂) / l₂.

Tip: drag along the wire to get close, then use the slider and the −1 / −0.1 / +0.1 / +1 buttons for the final approach. The needle gets closer to zero as you near the balance point.

The idea

The wire has a uniform potential gradient k (volts per cm). At the null point the galvanometer reads zero, so the potential across the length l equals the cell's voltage.

  • K₂ open: E = k · l₁
  • K₂ closed: V = k · l₂   (V = E − I r, I = E/(R + r))
  • Dividing: r = R (l₁ − l₂) / l₂

    Precautions

    • Connect the positive terminals of the driver cell and the test cell to the same end (A) of the wire.
    • The driver cell's emf must be larger than the test cell's, otherwise no balance point exists.
    • Keep the key closed only while taking a reading, so the cells do not run down.
    • Use a high series resistance with the galvanometer while searching for the balance point, then remove it for the final adjustment.
    • Do not slide the jockey along the wire; tap it at each position.
