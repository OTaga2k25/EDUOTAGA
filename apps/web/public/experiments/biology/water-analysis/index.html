Water analysis: pH, clarity and living organisms

Water from a tap, a pond and a drain is tested for pH, clarity (how deep you can see through it) and the living organisms in a drop. Compare the three and judge which is polluted.

The tests

  • pH: universal indicator paper; polluted water with decaying matter is often acidic
  • Clarity: the depth at which a black cross under the tube just disappears; a smaller depth means more turbid water
  • Organisms: a drop under the microscope; clean ponds have many kinds of algae and animals
  • Organic pollution feeds bacteria that use up dissolved oxygen (high BOD), so few kinds survive

    Precautions

    • Collect samples in clean, labelled bottles and test them soon after collection.
    • Shake gently before taking a sample so that organisms and particles are evenly spread.
    • Look straight down the tube in good, even light when judging the cross.
    • Wash your hands after handling drain or pond water.
