The idea

For a metallic conductor at constant temperature the current is proportional to the potential difference across it (Ohm's law). The ratio V/I is the resistance R, and it does not depend on the current.

  • Single resistor: V = I R, so R = V / I (slope of the V–I graph)
  • In series: Rs = R₁ + R₂
  • In parallel: 1/Rp = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂, or Rp = R₁R₂ / (R₁ + R₂)

Precautions

  • Connect the ammeter in series and the voltmeter in parallel, with the positive terminals towards the positive of the cell.
  • Check the zero error of both meters before starting.
  • Keep the key closed only while taking a reading, so that the resistor does not heat up and change its resistance.
  • Use the rheostat to keep the current small enough that the resistor does not become hot.
  • Take readings with the needle well deflected, not near the zero of the scale, to reduce the percentage error.
  • A real voltmeter draws a small current, so the measured resistance is very slightly less than the true value.
