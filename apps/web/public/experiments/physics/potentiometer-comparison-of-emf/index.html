Potentiometer: comparing the emfs of two cells

Balance each cell in turn on the potentiometer wire. The potential drop is proportional to length, so E₁ / E₂ = l₁ / l₂.

Tip: drag along the wire to get close, then use the slider and the −1 / −0.1 / +0.1 / +1 buttons for the final approach. The needle gets closer to zero as you near the balance point.

The idea

The wire has a uniform potential gradient k (volts per cm). At the null point the galvanometer reads zero, no current flows through the cell, and its emf equals the potential drop along the balance length.

  • Cell E₁ connected: E₁ = k · l₁
  • Cell E₂ connected: E₂ = k · l₂
  • Dividing: E₁ / E₂ = l₁ / l₂

Changing the rheostat changes k, so both lengths change, but their ratio does not. That is why the experiment is repeated for several rheostat settings.

    Precautions

    • Connect the positive terminals of the driver cell and of both test cells to the same end (A) of the wire.
    • The driver cell's emf must be larger than the emf of either test cell, otherwise no balance point exists.
    • Use the two-way key so that only one cell is connected to the galvanometer at a time.
    • Keep the key closed only while taking a reading, so the cells do not run down.
    • Do not slide the jockey along the wire; tap it at each position.
    • Do not change the rheostat while the two lengths of one set are being measured.
