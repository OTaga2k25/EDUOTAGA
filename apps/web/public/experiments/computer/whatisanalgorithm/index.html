Theory & Fundamentals

  

    
What is an Algorithm?

    
An algorithm is a finite, ordered sequence of well-defined instructions used to solve a specific problem or perform a computation. Every algorithm must have:

    
    
      
  • Input — the problem data
    • 
      
  • Output — the desired result
    • 
      
  • Definiteness — every step is precisely defined
    • 
      
  • Finiteness — it terminates after a finite number of steps
    • 
      
  • Effectiveness — every step can be carried out
    • 
    

    
In this experiment, the robot's instructions form an algorithm. The input is the warehouse layout and the package position. The output is the package delivered to the drop zone. Each instruction is definite (Move Forward, Turn Right, etc.), the sequence is finite (6 steps), and each step is effective (the robot can execute it).

  


  

    
Why Instruction Order Matters

    
An algorithm's correctness depends on the sequence of its instructions. Even with the right instructions, a wrong order produces incorrect results — or crashes. This is why the "wrong algorithm" demonstration fails: the robot turns too early and collides with a wall.

Conclusion

  

    

      An algorithm is more than just a list of commands—it is a precise, ordered, and finite sequence of steps designed to solve a specific problem. As demonstrated by the warehouse robot, even if individual instructions are valid, executing them in the wrong order will fail to produce the correct Output from the given Input. Correctness and sequence are fundamental to algorithmic problem-solving.
