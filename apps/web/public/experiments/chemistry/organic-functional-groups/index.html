Organic analysis: identify the functional group

Do the preliminary tests, follow where they point, and confirm the functional group: alcohol, phenol, aldehyde, ketone, carboxylic acid or primary aromatic amine. Record each test as experiment – observation – inference.

The tests

  • Carboxylic acid: blue litmus → red; NaHCO₃ → brisk effervescence (CO₂); ester test → fruity smell
  • Phenol: neutral FeCl₃ → violet; bromine water → white ppt
  • Aldehyde: 2,4-DNP → yellow-orange ppt; Tollens’ → silver mirror; Schiff’s → pink; Fehling’s → brick-red ppt (aliphatic only)
  • Ketone: 2,4-DNP → yellow-orange ppt; no silver mirror; sodium nitroprusside + NaOH → red
  • Alcohol: sodium → H₂; ceric ammonium nitrate → yellow to red
  • Primary aromatic amine: dissolves in dil. HCl; azo dye test → orange-red dye; carbylamine test → offensive smell
  • Iodoform test (yellow ppt) is shown by CH₃CO– and CH₃CH(OH)– compounds: ethanol, acetaldehyde, acetone

Check your work

    Precautions

    • Use small quantities of the compound and reagents, in clean, dry test tubes.
    • Organic compounds are often flammable: heat them in a water bath, not over a naked flame.
    • Handle sodium metal with forceps and use only a tiny piece, in a dry test tube.
    • Do the carbylamine test in a fume cupboard: the isocyanide is poisonous. Destroy it afterwards with conc. HCl.
    • Tollens’ reagent must be freshly prepared, and the silver mirror washed away with dil. HNO₃ afterwards.
    • Report the functional group only after its confirmatory test.
