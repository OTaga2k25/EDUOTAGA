Galvanometer: resistance by half deflection and figure of merit

Pass a small current through a galvanometer with a high resistance R and note the deflection θ. Then shunt it with S until the deflection is halved. The galvanometer resistance is G = RS / (R − S) and its figure of merit is k = E / ((R + G) θ).

Close K₁ to pass current through R and the galvanometer. K₂ connects the shunt S across the galvanometer.

  1. 1 K₂ open: choose R for a large deflection θ and record it
  2. 2 Close K₂: adjust S until the deflection is θ/2 and record S

The idea

With K₂ open, the current through the galvanometer is I = E / (R + G) and it gives a deflection θ. The figure of merit k is the current needed for one division, so k = I/θ = E / ((R + G) θ).

When the shunt S is connected across the galvanometer and adjusted so the deflection falls to θ/2, the galvanometer carries half its earlier current. Working this out gives G = RS / (R − S). Because R is very large compared with S, G ≈ S.

  • Galvanometer resistance: G = RS / (R − S)
  • Figure of merit: k = E / ((R + G) θ) ampere per division
  • Choose R so that θ is large (20–30 div) and preferably even, so θ/2 is easy to read

Check your readings

    Precautions

    • Before closing K₁, set R to a high value (several thousand ohms), or the galvanometer can be damaged by a large current.
    • Choose R so that the deflection is large, preferably an even number of divisions, so that θ/2 is a whole number.
    • Close K₂ only after recording θ, and do not change R while finding the half deflection.
    • Read the deflection without parallax, with your eye directly above the pointer.
    • Take readings for at least four values of R and find the mean of G and of k.
