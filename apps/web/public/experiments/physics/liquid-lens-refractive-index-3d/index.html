The idea

Light from the needle passes through the lens, reflects from the mirror and passes through the lens again. When the needle is at the focus of the lens, the rays leave the lens parallel, return along their own path and form an inverted image exactly at the needle. Then there is no parallax: the image tip stays on the needle tip as you move your eye. The distance from the needle to the lens is then its focal length.

A few drops of liquid between the lens and the mirror form a plano-concave liquid lens in contact with the convex lens. The combination has focal length F, larger than f₁.

  • Focal length = mean of the needle's distance to the top of the lens (x₁) and to the mirror (x₂)
  • Lenses in contact: 1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/fl, so |fl| = f₁F / (F − f₁)
  • Liquid lens (one face radius R, the other flat): 1/|fl| = (n − 1)/R, so n = 1 + R/|fl|
  • Spherometer: R = l²/(6h) + h/2, where l is the distance between the legs and h the height of the central screw
  • Parallax rule: of the needle and the image, the one farther from your eye moves with your eye

    Precautions

    • The plane mirror and the lens must be clean, and the needle must be vertically above the centre of the lens.
    • Remove parallax tip to tip: keep your eye at least 30 cm above the needle and move it sideways.
    • Use only a few drops of liquid, so it forms a thin layer just under the lens without spreading over its edge.
    • Measure x₁ and x₂ carefully and take their mean, which is the distance to the middle of the lens.
    • Repeat each setting at least twice and take the mean.
