Galvanometer: conversion into a voltmeter and an ammeter

A galvanometer gives full-scale deflection for a tiny current Ig = N k. With a high resistance R = V₀/Ig − G in series it becomes a voltmeter of range V₀. With a low shunt S = IgG/(I₀ − Ig) in parallel it becomes an ammeter of range I₀.

The idea

A galvanometer of resistance G gives full-scale deflection (N divisions) for a current Ig = N k, where k is its figure of merit. This is usually below a milliampere.

  • Voltmeter of range V₀: the full-scale current must flow when V₀ is applied, so Ig(G + R) = V₀, giving R = V₀/Ig − G (a high resistance in series)
  • Ammeter of range I₀: only Ig flows through the galvanometer and the rest through the shunt, so IgG = (I₀ − Ig)S, giving S = IgG/(I₀ − Ig) (a low resistance in parallel)
  • Shunt wire: length l = S/ρ, where ρ is the resistance per centimetre of the wire
  • The converted scale reads (θ/N) × full range

    Precautions

    • Find G and k carefully first (half-deflection method), since every later value depends on them.
    • The series resistance for a voltmeter is large: check the resistance box plugs are tight.
    • The shunt wire is thick and short: measure its length between the binding screws accurately, and remove the insulation at the ends.
    • Connect the converted voltmeter in parallel with the standard voltmeter, and the converted ammeter in series with the standard ammeter.
    • Start with the rheostat at its safe end so that the meters are not overloaded.
