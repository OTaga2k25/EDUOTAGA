Acid–base titration: estimating the strength of a solution

A known volume of acid is neutralised by a base run in from a burette. Phenolphthalein turns from colourless to pale pink at the end point. From the volumes, M₁V₁/n₁ = M₂V₂/n₂ gives the molarity, and strength = molarity × molar mass.

  1. 1 Rinse and fill the burette
  2. 2 Pipette 20 mL acid, add indicator
  3. 3 Note the initial reading
  4. 4 Titrate to pale pink, note the final reading

The idea

At the end point the acid in the flask is exactly neutralised. One more drop of base makes the solution slightly alkaline, and phenolphthalein turns pale pink.

  • Oxalic acid with alkali: H₂C₂O₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂C₂O₄ + 2H₂O (n₁ = 1, n₂ = 2)
  • Hydrochloric acid with NaOH: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (n₁ = n₂ = 1)
  • Molarity equation: M₁V₁/n₁ = M₂V₂/n₂ (1 = acid, 2 = base)
  • Strength (g/L) = molarity × molar mass (NaOH 40, KOH 56, HCl 36.5)
  • Use the concordant titre: two titres that agree within 0.1 mL

    Precautions

    • Rinse the burette with the solution it is to hold, and the pipette with the solution it is to measure. Rinse the conical flask with distilled water only.
    • Remove the air bubble from the burette nozzle before taking the initial reading, and remove the funnel.
    • Read the bottom of the meniscus with your eye at the same level (to avoid parallax).
    • Do a rough titration first, then add the base drop by drop near the end point while swirling the flask.
    • Place the flask on a white tile so that the first permanent pale pink is easy to see.
    • Repeat until two titres agree within 0.1 mL (concordant values).
