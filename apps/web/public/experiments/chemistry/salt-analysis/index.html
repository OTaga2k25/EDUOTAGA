The scheme

  • NH₄⁺: warm with NaOH → smell of ammonia, red litmus blue; Nessler’s reagent → brown ppt
  • CO₃²⁻: dil. H₂SO₄ → brisk effervescence; gas turns lime water milky
  • CH₃COO⁻: dil. H₂SO₄ → vinegar smell; neutral FeCl₃ → deep red; ester test → fruity smell
  • Cl⁻: conc. H₂SO₄ → white fumes with NH₄OH rod; AgNO₃ → curdy white ppt soluble in NH₄OH
  • NO₃⁻: conc. H₂SO₄ + Cu → brown fumes; brown ring test
  • SO₄²⁻: BaCl₂ → white ppt insoluble in conc. HCl
  • Cation groups: I dil. HCl (Pb²⁺) · III NH₄Cl + NH₄OH (Al³⁺) · IV H₂S in ammoniacal solution (Zn²⁺) · V (NH₄)₂CO₃ (Ba²⁺, Ca²⁺) · VI Na₂HPO₄ (Mg²⁺)

    Precautions

    • Use clean test tubes, and only a small amount of salt for each test.
    • Smell gases by wafting them towards your nose, never directly.
    • Add conc. H₂SO₄ carefully, and point the test tube away from yourself and others while heating.
    • Do the group tests in order (I → VI): ions of an earlier group also precipitate with later group reagents.
    • Report an ion only after a confirmatory test.
