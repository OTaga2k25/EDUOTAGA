Tangent galvanometer: reduction factor and the Earth's horizontal field

A current in a circular coil makes a field B = μ₀nI/2r at its centre, at right angles to the Earth's horizontal field BH. The compass needle settles at θ with tan θ = B/BH. So I = K tan θ, with reduction factor K = 2rBH/μ₀n.

The idea

The coil is set in the magnetic meridian, so its field at the centre (along the axis) is perpendicular to BH. The needle sets along the resultant of the two fields.

  • Field of the coil at its centre: B = μ₀ n I / 2r
  • Tangent law: B = BH tan θ, so I = K tan θ with K = 2r BH/(μ₀ n)
  • Reduction factor from readings: K = I / tan θ; then BH = μ₀ n K / 2r
  • Radius from the measured circumference: r = C / 2π
  • Most sensitive near θ = 45°: choose the current (and turns) so θ is between 30° and 60°

    Precautions

    • Keep magnets, iron objects and current-carrying wires away from the tangent galvanometer.
    • Set the plane of the coil in the magnetic meridian: with no current, turn the instrument until the pointer reads 0–0.
    • Level the instrument, so the needle moves freely in a horizontal plane.
    • Read both ends of the pointer (to remove eccentricity error) and reverse the current (to remove error from imperfect setting in the meridian).
    • Adjust the current so that the deflection is between 30° and 60°.
    • Tap the box gently before reading to overcome friction at the pivot.
