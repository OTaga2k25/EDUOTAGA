Quadrat method: population density, frequency and abundance

Counting every plant in a field is impossible, so ecologists sample it. Place 1 m × 1 m quadrats at random, count each species inside them, and estimate density, frequency and abundance.

Count the plants in the quadrat

Count a plant only if its base (the dark dot) is inside the frame.

The idea

  • Density = total number of individuals of the species ÷ total number of quadrats studied (per m²)
  • Frequency (%) = number of quadrats in which the species occurs ÷ total quadrats studied × 100
  • Abundance = total number of individuals ÷ number of quadrats in which it occurs
  • Quadrats are placed at random so that the sample represents the whole area

    Precautions

    • Place the quadrats at random over the whole area, not only where plants look interesting.
    • Study a sufficient number of quadrats (at least 10); quadrats must not overlap.
    • Count a plant only if it is rooted inside the quadrat; decide the rule for plants on the edge and follow it every time.
    • Identify each species carefully before counting; count each plant only once.
