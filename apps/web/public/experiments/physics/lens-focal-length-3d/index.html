The idea

A convex lens forms a real, inverted image on a screen when the object is beyond its focal length. The distances are measured from the lens. With the object at distance u and the sharp image at distance v, the lens formula holds for every position.

  • Lens formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f (with the sign convention, u is negative)
  • In magnitudes: 1/u + 1/v = 1/f, so f = uv / (u + v)
  • Graph of 1/v against 1/u: a straight line with intercept 1/f on both axes

    Precautions

    • The lens, the object and the screen must be at the same height, with their centres on one straight line (the optical axis).
    • The tip of the object needle or arrow must lie on the axis of the lens, and the lens must be placed upright, perpendicular to the bench.
    • Keep u greater than f. If the object is inside the focal length no real image forms on the screen.
    • Find the position of best focus by moving the screen a little each side of the sharpest image and taking the middle.
    • Avoid values of u very close to f, where v becomes very large, and values near 2f where small errors matter more. Take readings between 1.5 f and 3 f.
    • Perform the experiment in a darkened room, with the object brightly lit, so that the image is easy to see.
