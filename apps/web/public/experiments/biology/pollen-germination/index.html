Pollen germination on a slide: effect of sucrose concentration

Pollen grains sown in a sugar solution put out pollen tubes, as they would on a stigma. Count the germinated grains in different sucrose concentrations and find the one that suits them best: % germination = germinated ÷ total × 100.

Medium

Tap each germinated grain to mark it (tap again to unmark).

The idea

  • A grain has germinated when its pollen tube is at least as long as the grain is wide
  • % germination = germinated grains ÷ total grains × 100
  • Sucrose: food for the growing tube and an osmotic balance for the grain
  • In water grains burst (water enters); in strong sugar solution water is drawn out
  • Boric acid (boron) helps the pollen tube to grow

    Precautions

    • Use pollen from freshly opened flowers (for example Hibiscus, Balsam or Vinca).
    • Use clean slides and freshly prepared solutions.
    • Dust the pollen thinly so that grains do not overlap.
    • Keep the slide in a moist chamber (a Petri dish lined with wet blotting paper) so that it does not dry.
    • Count all the grains in a field, using the same rule for “germinated” every time.
