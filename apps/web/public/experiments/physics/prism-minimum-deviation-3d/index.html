The idea

Light bends towards the normal on entering the glass and away from it on leaving, so the ray is turned towards the base of the prism. The angle between the incident ray and the emergent ray is the angle of deviation δ. It depends on the angle of incidence i.

  • Deviation: δ = i + e − A (e is the angle of emergence)
  • At minimum deviation: i = e and r₁ = r₂ = A/2, and the ray inside is parallel to the base
  • Refractive index: n = sin((A + D)/2) / sin(A/2), where D is the minimum deviation

Precautions

  • Fix the prism on the drawing board with its refracting edge perpendicular to the paper, and mark its outline before removing it.
  • Use bright, thin pins (or a narrow beam) and keep the ray close to the base of the prism where it passes through the glass.
  • Take readings for i from about 35° to 70° so that the curve falls, turns and rises again, with at least five points near the minimum.
  • Near the minimum the emergent ray hardly moves, so take readings at small steps there and fit the smooth curve through the points.
  • If the angle of incidence is too small, the ray cannot leave the second face because of total internal reflection. Increase i.
  • Read the deviation at the lowest point of the curve, not from the lowest single reading.
