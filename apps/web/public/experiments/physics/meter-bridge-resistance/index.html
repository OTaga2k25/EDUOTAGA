Meter bridge: resistance and resistivity of a wire

Balance a known resistance R against an unknown wire X on a one-metre wire. At the null point X = R (100 − l) / l, and the resistivity follows from ρ = X π d² / 4L.

Tip: drag along the wire to get close, then use the slider and the −1 / −0.1 / +0.1 / +1 buttons for the final approach. A balance point near the middle of the wire (about 50 cm) gives the most accurate X.

The idea

The four resistances R, X and the two parts of the wire form a Wheatstone bridge. The wire is uniform, so the resistance of each part is proportional to its length. At the null point no current flows through the galvanometer and the resistances are in proportion.

  • Balance: R / X = l / (100 − l)
  • Unknown resistance: X = R (100 − l) / l
  • Resistivity: ρ = X A / L = X π d² / 4L

Precautions

  • All connections must be clean and tight, and the copper strips must be free from oxide.
  • Choose R so that the balance point lies near the middle of the wire (between 40 cm and 60 cm), where the percentage error is smallest.
  • Keep the key closed only while taking a reading, so that the wire does not heat up and change its resistance.
  • Tap the jockey on the wire; do not slide it along the wire.
  • Interchanging R and X and repeating the reading removes the error due to the end resistances of the bridge.
  • Measure the diameter of the unknown wire at several places and in two perpendicular directions with a screw gauge, and take the mean.
