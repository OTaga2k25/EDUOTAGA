Salivary amylase: effect of temperature and pH on starch digestion

Salivary amylase digests starch into maltose. Iodine turns starch blue-black; when all the starch is digested the drop keeps the yellowish iodine colour: the achromic point. The sooner it is reached, the faster the enzyme works: rate = 1/time.

Mark the achromic point (the drop stays yellowish)

The idea

  • Starch → (salivary amylase) → maltose
  • Iodine + starch: blue-black; partly digested (dextrins): violet to reddish brown; no starch: yellowish
  • Achromic point: the first test in which iodine gives no blue colour
  • Rate of enzyme action ∝ 1 / time to the achromic point
  • Optimum: about 37 °C and pH 6.8; heat denatures the enzyme, cold only slows it

    Precautions

    • Rinse your mouth before collecting saliva, and dilute it with distilled water.
    • Keep each tube at its temperature for a few minutes before adding saliva, and add saliva to all tubes at the same time.
    • Use a clean dropper for each tube so that the solutions do not mix.
    • Take drops at regular, equal intervals and compare colours against a white background.
    • Use buffer solutions to keep the pH constant.
