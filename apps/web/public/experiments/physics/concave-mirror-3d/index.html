The idea

A concave mirror forms a real, inverted image in front of it when the object is beyond the focus. The object and the screen are on the same side of the mirror, and both distances are measured from the pole of the mirror. Here the screen covers only the part below the axis, where the inverted image falls, so it does not block the light from the object.

  • Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f (with the sign convention, u, v and f are all negative)
  • In magnitudes: f = uv / (u + v), and the radius of curvature R = 2f
  • When u = v, object and image are both at the centre of curvature: u = v = 2f
  • Graph of 1/v against 1/u: a straight line with intercept 1/f on both axes

    Precautions

    • The mirror, the object and the screen must be at the same height, with the pole of the mirror and the base of the object on the principal axis.
    • The mirror should face the object squarely and stand upright.
    • Keep u greater than f. If the object is inside the focal length, only a virtual image forms behind the mirror and nothing appears on the screen.
    • Find the position of best focus by moving the screen a little each side of the sharpest image and taking the middle.
    • Find the rough focal length first by focusing a distant window on the screen, then take readings with u between about 1.5 f and 3 f.
    • Perform the experiment in a darkened room with a brightly lit object.
